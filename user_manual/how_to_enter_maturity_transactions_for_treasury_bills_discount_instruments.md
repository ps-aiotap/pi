Want to enter a maturity transaction for Treasury Bills or Discount
Instruments? Follow the below steps for the same.

**Enter maturity transactions for Treasury Bills/Discount
Instruments.\**

There are two methods to enter maturity transactions for Treasury
Bills/Discount Instruments:

**Method 1**

The first method is to journal the gain to interest income through a
journal entry.

**Step 1 **

Go to **Menu **\> **Transactions **\> **Fixed Income**.

**Step 2 **

Enter a maturity transaction for the Treasury Bill or Discount
Instrument.

*Tip: You can either add a new transaction or copy an existing
transaction.\*

*Note: The Net Amount will be the maturity value of the investment. In
most cases, this is the face or par value of the discounted investment.*

![](media/image1.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.5256944444444445in"}

This transaction results in a capital gain because you may have entered
the purchase transaction at a discounted price and maturity transaction
at face or par value. The system considers the difference between the
two as a capital gain.

![](media/image2.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.204861111111111in"}

This capital gain needs to be moved to an interest income account using
a journal entry.

**Step 3 **

Go to **Menu **\> **Transactions **\> **Journal Entry**.

**Step 4 **

Do a Journal Entry transaction from short term capital gain to interest
income.

![](media/image3.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.2916666666666665in"}

![](media/image4.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.134027777777778in"}

**Method 2**

The second method is to have the US Treasury Bill/Discount Instrument
mature at cost and then create an interest payout transaction for the
difference between cost and maturity.

**Step 1**

**Menu **\> **Transactions **\> **Fixed Income**.

**Step 2 **

Go to the Fixed Income security that has matured.

**Step 3 **

Enter the maturity transaction at the cost of which the security was
bought. Typically, this amount would be the discounted price that the
investment was bought at. This does not create any capital gain as the
purchase and maturity amount is the same.

![](media/image5.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.5256944444444445in"}

![](media/image6.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.2888888888888888in"}

**Step 4 **

Enter an interest payout transaction with the amount being the
difference between cost and maturity.

height="3.5256944444444445in"}

![](media/image8.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
