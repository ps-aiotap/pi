Want to enter a taxable merger? Follow the below steps for the same.

First let's differentiate between a taxable and non-taxable merger.

**Difference between Taxable and Non-taxable merger**

The difference between a taxable and a non-taxable merger is that in a
non-taxable merger the cost is carried over from the previous stock to
the new stock. In a taxable merger the cost basis becomes the market
value of the old stock less the cash received; the tax date becomes the
current date (transaction date).

Let\'s take an example!

We are going to do a merger between Apple and Microsoft. Microsoft is
going to take over Apple. And the terms of the deal as announced is that
on April 12th for every share of Apple, you will get 0.1 share of
Microsoft and \$133 in cash.

**Step 1 **

*Note: Before entering any data, you first want to check and make sure
that the merger makes economic sense. In terms of the efficient market,
whatever value you give up should be the value of what you get. \*

In our example, the day before the merger (April 11^th^) we own 535
shares of Apple worth \$86,208 (what is Microsoft on April 11^th^\].

The day of the merger (April 12^th^) we are going to be receiving 0.1
shares of Microsoft for every share of Apple that we own. 

535 shares of Apple X 0.1 shares of Microsoft = 53.5 shares of Microsoft
that we receive.

On April 11th Microsoft is trading at 282.83, so the market value of our
Microsoft stock is \$15,131.41 (53.5 X 282.83= \$15,131.31).

We will also be receiving \$133 dollars for every share of Apple that we
previously owned.

So, the cash we will be receiving is 535 X 133= \$71,155.

If you add up the total of what you are receiving from the new Microsoft
stock + the cash, it should be roughly what you are giving up from
Apple.

\$15,131.41 + \$71,155= \$86,286.41.

If for instance, you added up the cash and the new stock and the total
was \$82,000. You are going to want to go back and review as that
discrepancy is relatively large.

**Step 2**

On April 12^th^ you are going to sell Apple for the total value of the
merger (\$86,286.81).

In the Notes section, you can add "Merger of AAPL with MSFT for .1
shares + 133 in cash" so when you go back you can remember why that sale
was made.

**Step 3 **

You are going to buy Microsoft on April 12^th^.

So, in the example we have been using you would purchase 53.5 shares for
a market value of \$15,131.31.

![](media/image2.jpeg){width="6.268055555555556in"
height="3.5180555555555557in"}

**Step 4 **

You now need to sell the .5 fractional share from the 53.5 shares you
received.

Typically, the bank will send you "cash in lieu" instead of .5 shares of
a stock.

What "cash in lieu" really means is a sale of the .5 share.

In our example, the bank sent us \$140.25 cash in lieu.

The way to process this in Asset Vantage is by doing a sale transaction
one day after the merger.

On April 13^th^, enter a sale transaction for .5 shares at a market
value of \$140.25. In the Notes section enter "Cash in lieu from
merger".
