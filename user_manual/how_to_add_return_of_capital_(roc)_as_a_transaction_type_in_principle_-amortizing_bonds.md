**Importance of Return of Capital (ROC):** 

When you hold principal-amortizing bonds---also called self-liquidating
bonds---you'll notice they return small portions of your invested
principal at regular intervals before maturity. These payments are
called Return of Capital (ROC). 

**Each time you receive an ROC: **

- The bond's face value and cost basis automatically reduce, in the same
  proportion. 

<!-- -->

- Your quantity stays the same, so there\'s no change in how many units
  you hold. 

<!-- -->

- The interest you earn going forward is recalculated based on the
  updated face value. 

<!-- -->

- All related reports---like market value, yield, and unrealized
  P&L---reflect these changes accurately. 

If ROC isn't recorded, your reports can show inflated values or outdated
yield calculations. So, booking it correctly helps ensure your
performance metrics stay clean and reliable.

*Return of Capital (ROC) will be visible as a Transaction Type under

**Steps:**

2.  Select Instrument Type as **Fixed Income** → **Bonds & Debenture**.

3.  In the Transaction Type dropdown, select** Return of Capital
    (ROC)**.
